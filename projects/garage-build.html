PROJECT

2-Story Garage Build

Structural design and construction of a two-story garage combining protected vehicle storage with residential accommodations, developed through SolidWorks, FEA, material optimization, contractor coordination, and construction planning.

Structural Design SolidWorks + FEA Construction Coordination

Overview

This project followed a similar process to the 40 × 60 × 18 ft steel shop build, beginning with the structure and space layout in SolidWorks before construction. The design had to support two different uses: protected vehicle storage on the first floor and a functional residential space on the second floor.

The upper level was planned with two bedrooms, a full-size bathroom, and a kitchen for additional family accommodations. Structural performance, material usage, room dimensions, utility routing, construction sequence, contractor timing, and budget all had to be considered together during the design and build.

Project Scope

Took the project from CAD layout and structural analysis through material planning, contractor coordination, construction scheduling, and residential space development.

Design & Engineering

  • Developed the building layout and structural design in SolidWorks before construction
  • Used FEA to evaluate structural members under wind loads, snow loads, structural weight, and roof loading from solar panels
  • Compared material sizes and layouts to achieve structural requirements while controlling cost and reducing waste
  • Designed bedroom dimensions, wall placement, and staircase geometry around efficient use of standard construction materials
  • Balanced protected vehicle storage on the first floor with a functional residential layout above

Tools & Skills

SolidWorks FEA Structural Design Material Optimization Construction Planning Project Scheduling Contractor Coordination Cost Control Space Planning

Selected Work

01

SolidWorks Building Design

The project began with a complete building and space layout in SolidWorks. The model was used to establish overall dimensions, structural arrangement, vehicle clearances, room sizes, staircase placement, and the relationship between the first-floor garage and second-floor living space.

02

Structural Analysis & FEA

Structural members were evaluated using FEA and engineering calculations. Loading considerations included wind forces, structural dead load, snow accumulation, and the added roof loading created by the solar-panel system.

03

Material Optimization

Material selection was based on both strength and efficiency. Member sizes, spans, wall locations, and construction dimensions were evaluated to meet structural requirements while controlling material cost and reducing unnecessary waste.

04

Space & Material Planning

Bedroom dimensions, wall spacing, and staircase geometry were planned around standard material sizes to reduce cutting, offcuts, and unnecessary material usage while keeping the living space practical.

05

Vehicle Protection & First-Floor Layout

The first floor was developed primarily as protected vehicle storage, providing shelter from severe weather, storms, and long-term sun exposure while maintaining practical access and usable garage space.

06

Residential Second Floor

The second floor was designed with two bedrooms, a full-size bathroom, and a kitchen to provide additional accommodations when extended family visits.

07

Contractor Coordination

Coordinated plumbing, HVAC, and tile contractors so each trade could complete its work in the proper construction sequence without creating avoidable delays or rework.

08

Schedule & Budget Management

Construction sequencing, contractor timing, and project spending were tracked throughout the build. Scheduling decisions were made with both progress and budget in mind to help keep the project on track.

Engineering Takeaway

Like the 40 × 60 steel shop, this project required taking a building from a SolidWorks concept into a real structure. The residential requirements introduced another level of design consideration because structural performance had to be balanced with room dimensions, stairs, utilities, material efficiency, contractor sequencing, and overall cost.

The project reinforced how engineering decisions made during the design phase directly affect fabrication, construction efficiency, material waste, project scheduling, and the usability of the finished structure.
