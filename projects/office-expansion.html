PROJECT

Two-Story Shop & Office Expansion

Expansion of the existing shop with a two-story, climate-controlled workspace designed for engineering work, additive manufacturing, laser cutting, vinyl equipment, office use, and future technical growth.

Facility Expansion SolidWorks + FEA HVAC · Ventilation · Electrical

Overview

As our equipment and workspace needs continued to grow, doing most office and design work from bedrooms became increasingly cramped. The problem was a positive one: the operation was growing, but the available space was no longer keeping up.

The solution was to expand the existing shop to the side and create a two-story office and technical workspace. The new addition is intended to relocate equipment such as 3D printers, laser cutters, vinyl printers and cutters, and other sensitive equipment into a dedicated, climate-controlled area close to the main shop.

Design Goal

Create additional technical workspace that feels like a seamless extension of the existing shop while supporting sensitive equipment, controlled temperature, ventilation, plumbing, electrical growth, and long-term expansion.

Design & Engineering

  • Designed the addition in SolidWorks before construction began
  • Matched the proportions, wall heights, and overall style of the existing shop so the expansion would blend in visually and structurally
  • Used FEA to evaluate structural performance under weather loads and other expected forces
  • Planned HVAC and insulation around sensitive equipment and tighter climate-control requirements
  • Designed ventilation paths for equipment that can produce fumes, heat, or airborne contaminants during operation
  • Planned plumbing, window placement, weather sealing, and electrical growth as part of the building layout

Tools & Skills

Selected Work

01

Growth Problem & Space Planning

As additional equipment was added, the existing office setup became too cramped to support design work and expanding fabrication capabilities. The project began by defining which equipment needed to remain near the shop but would benefit from a cleaner and more controlled environment.

02

SolidWorks Building Design

The two-story addition was laid out in SolidWorks before construction. The model was used to establish floor layout, wall positions, structural geometry, window locations, equipment areas, and the interface between the new addition and the existing shop.

03

Structural Integration & FEA

The addition was designed around the same general proportions and visual language as the existing shop. FEA and engineering analysis were used to evaluate the structure under weather loads and other forces while maintaining compatibility with the existing building height and wall geometry.

04

Climate-Controlled Equipment Space

The expansion provides a more appropriate environment for 3D printers, laser cutters, vinyl printing and cutting equipment, electronics, computers, and other equipment that benefits from cleaner air, stable temperatures, and protection from shop dust and temperature swings.

05

Ventilation & Safety Planning

Ventilation layout was considered for equipment that can generate fumes, heat, or airborne contaminants. The goal was to provide controlled exhaust paths while preserving the climate-controlled environment of the workspace and separating process exhaust from occupied areas.

06

Windows, Trim & Weather Sealing

Window placement and exterior trim details were planned to reduce the risk of water intrusion. The building envelope was designed to stay well sealed so HVAC performance and indoor climate control would not be compromised by uncontrolled air leakage or moisture.

07

HVAC & Plumbing Integration

The addition was planned to accommodate HVAC and plumbing without interfering with structural members or usable workspace. Routing and equipment placement were considered early so these systems could be incorporated without unnecessary rework later in construction.

08

Electrical Capacity for Future Growth

Electrical planning accounted for a growing number of printers, cutters, computers, fabrication equipment, and future tools. The layout was developed to provide practical outlet locations and sufficient capacity for the workspace to continue expanding without immediately outgrowing the electrical system.

Engineering Takeaway

This project was not simply about creating more square footage. The challenge was to expand an existing working shop without making the addition feel separate or improvised. Structural geometry, appearance, wall heights, windows, weather sealing, utilities, climate control, ventilation, and electrical capacity all had to work together.

It reinforced the importance of planning building systems early in the design process. A workspace intended for technical equipment has different requirements than ordinary office space, and those requirements need to be considered before construction begins rather than added afterward.
